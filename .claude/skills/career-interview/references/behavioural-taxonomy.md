# Behavioural interview taxonomy

Every accomplishment and story in the profile gets classified against the questions behavioural interviewers actually ask — "tell me about a time you had to handle conflict", "tell me about a time you failed". The CV gets someone into the room; the story bank is what they answer from once they're there. Building it during the interview costs a line per accomplishment. Rebuilding it the night before an interview means trying to remember stories cold, which is exactly the recall problem this whole process exists to solve.

## The categories

Use the tag in the profile; it keeps the story bank index sortable. The question column is how interviewers phrase it, so the candidate recognises the slot when it comes up in a different wording.

| Tag | Competency | How it gets asked | What the interviewer is listening for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `conflict` | Conflict with a peer or team | "Tell me about a time you had to handle conflict at work." · "...a disagreement with a coworker." · "...worked with someone difficult." | They addressed it directly, understood the other side, reached a resolution, and the working relationship survived |
| `disagree-up` | Disagreeing with a manager or a decision | "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager." · "...pushed back on a decision." | Respectful challenge with evidence, then commitment, whichever way it went |
| `influence` | Influence without authority | "Tell me about a time you convinced someone to change their mind." · "...got buy-in for an idea." | How they built the case, tailored it to the audience, and moved people they didn't manage |
| `stakeholder` | Difficult stakeholder or customer | "Tell me about a time you dealt with a difficult stakeholder / client." | Understanding the underlying need, managing expectations, staying calm, the outcome for the relationship |
| `leadership` | Leading people or a project | "Tell me about a time you led a team / project." · "...stepped up to lead." | Setting direction, getting others to deliver, handling the people side |
| `ownership` | Initiative and ownership | "Tell me about a time you went above and beyond." · "...fixed something nobody asked you to." | Saw the problem, didn't wait for permission, saw it through to the end |
| `failure` | Failure or mistake | "Tell me about a time you failed." · "...made a mistake." · "...a project that didn't go as planned." | Real ownership with no deflection, what they did about it, what they changed afterwards |
| `feedback` | Receiving critical feedback | "Tell me about a time you received difficult feedback." | Didn't get defensive, acted on it, has evidence the change stuck |
| `ambiguity` | Ambiguity and incomplete information | "Tell me about a time you worked with unclear requirements." · "...had to act without all the information." | How they created structure, which assumptions they made explicit, how they reduced the uncertainty |
| `deadline` | Pressure and tight deadlines | "Tell me about a time you worked under a tight deadline." · "...under pressure." | What they cut or protected, how they communicated the risk, quality under pressure |
| `prioritisation` | Competing priorities | "How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?" · "Tell me about a time you had to say no." | An explicit method, the trade-off made, how they handled whoever lost out |
| `decision` | Hard decision or trade-off | "Tell me about a difficult decision you made." · "...used data to make a decision." | How they framed the options, the evidence they used, the cost they accepted, and how they'd judge it now |
| `problem-solving` | Complex problem | "Tell me about the hardest problem you've solved." · "...a technical challenge." | How they decomposed it, their hypotheses, the dead ends, the root cause rather than the symptom |
| `innovation` | Improvement or new idea | "Tell me about a time you improved a process." · "...came up with a creative solution." | What was wrong with the old way, why theirs was better, adoption, and the measured difference |
| `learning` | Learning fast | "Tell me about a time you had to learn something quickly." · "...worked outside your comfort zone." | How they learned it, how fast, and what they delivered with it |
| `adaptability` | Change | "Tell me about a time priorities changed suddenly." · "...went through a reorg." | Adjusted without losing momentum, and helped others adjust too |
| `communication` | Explaining the complex | "Tell me about a time you explained something technical to a non-technical audience." | Knowing the audience, choosing what to leave out, confirming they understood |
| `collaboration` | Cross-team work | "Tell me about a time you worked with another team to deliver something." | Shared goals, handoffs, credit-sharing, getting through the friction |
| `developing-others` | Mentoring and growing people | "Tell me about a time you helped someone grow." · "...delegated something important." | A specific person, what they did for that person, and where that person ended up |
| `customer` | Customer or user focus | "Tell me about a time you went out of your way for a customer / user." | Understanding the real need, what it cost them to meet it, and the customer outcome |
| `integrity` | Ethics and doing the right thing | "Tell me about a time you had to do the right thing at a cost." · "...raised a concern." | A real tension, a clear action, and accepting the consequence |
| `results` | Biggest impact | "What's your greatest professional achievement?" · "Tell me about a time you exceeded expectations." | Scale, their personal contribution, a number |

Extend the list when a target role has a recurring theme that doesn't fit, such as `crisis` for incident-heavy work or `scale` for hypergrowth companies. Don't rename the existing tags, because the index depends on them.

## How to classify

Do it silently, as you write each accomplishment to the file. Don't announce tags during Phase 3: the moment someone hears "that's a great conflict story", they switch from remembering to performing, which is the same failure as showing them polished bullets too early.

**Classify on what the story contains, not on what would be convenient.** A story only qualifies for `conflict` if there was a real disagreement with a real person and the candidate did something about it. A project with a tight timeline isn't automatically a `deadline` story unless the pressure shaped what they did. Interviewers drill into the slot they asked about ("what did the other person say?", "what would you do differently?"), and a stretched story collapses on the second follow-up, in the room, where it does the most damage.

**One primary tag, up to two secondary.** Most good stories answer several questions. The same migration story can be a `problem-solving` answer and an `influence` answer, but it gets told differently for each. So every tag carries an **angle**: one line saying which part of the story to foreground for that question.

> Behavioural tags:
> - Primary `influence`: getting the two other platform teams to adopt the schema they had already rejected once. Foreground the second pitch and what changed in it.
> - Secondary `problem-solving`: the root cause was clock skew, not the queue. Foreground the dead end with the queue.
> - Secondary `deadline`: shipped before the Q4 freeze. Thin, because the deadline didn't change what they did.

**Grade each tag**, not the story as a whole:

- **strong**: every part of STAR is present (Situation, Task, Action, Result), the action is clearly theirs, and the result is concrete. Ready to tell.
- **usable**: works, but one part is soft. Note which part.
- **thin**: the story touches the competency but can't carry the question alone. Note what's missing ("we never got the other person's view", "no outcome recorded").

The profile's Challenge/Action/Result maps directly onto STAR, with Challenge covering Situation and Task. `failure` and `feedback` answers also need a **Reflection**, meaning what they do differently now. Without it, a failure story reads as a confession rather than an answer.

## Stories that aren't accomplishments

Several of the most-asked questions (`conflict`, `failure`, `feedback`, `disagree-up`) rarely come out of an accomplishment-led role deep dive, because nobody puts them on a CV. They come out of Phase 5, side remarks in Phase 3, and the read-back's "oh, and also…".

When one turns up, capture it as a **standalone story** in the story bank: Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection, tags with angles and grades, and the role it belongs to. It doesn't need a number. It does need specifics: who the other person was (by role, not name), what was said, and what happened next.

## Coverage

These eight come up in almost every behavioural loop, so they are the **core set**: `conflict`, `failure`, `influence`, `leadership` or `ownership`, `deadline`, `disagree-up`, `ambiguity`, `results`.

- Aim for at least one **strong** story per core tag, and two for `conflict` and `failure`. Interviewers ask follow-ups like "tell me about another time", and those are the two questions where candidates most often have nothing prepared.
- **Spread the load.** If one signature project is the primary answer for four core tags, the candidate will tell it four times in one loop, and the interviewers compare notes afterwards. Find other stories so that no single story is the primary answer for more than two core tags.
- Prefer recent stories. Something from the last three years beats something from ten years ago, unless the old one is far stronger.
- Weight toward the target. Leadership-track roles lean on `leadership`, `developing-others` and `decision`. Client-facing roles lean on `stakeholder` and `customer`. Use the Phase 1b matrix to decide which non-core tags also need a strong story.

At the end of Phase 5, walk the core set. For each empty or thin row, ask one open question aimed at it (`question-bank.md` has the prompts). An honest "I can't think of one" goes in the bank as a real gap. It is worth knowing before an interviewer finds it.

## Company frameworks

Some employers publish the competencies they interview against (Amazon's Leadership Principles, for example). When a target company has one, map its labels onto these tags in the story bank index rather than inventing a parallel scheme: "Earn Trust" maps to `conflict` and `feedback`, "Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit" to `disagree-up`, "Dive Deep" to `problem-solving`, "Bias for Action" to `ambiguity` and `ownership`, and "Customer Obsession" to `customer`. Add the company label in the index's notes column.
