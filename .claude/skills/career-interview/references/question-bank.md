# Question bank

Raw material to draw from — not a script to read out. Pick the two or three questions that fit the person in front of you, ask them one at a time, and let their answers decide what comes next. If you find yourself working down a list in order, you have stopped interviewing.

## Phase 0 — artifact request

Send as one short message, framed as "anything you have, don't hunt for hours":

> Before we start, anything from this list that's easy to find will save us both a lot of time — and performance reviews in particular tend to be full of numbers people have forgotten they have:
> - your last two or three performance reviews or self-reviews
> - any promotion packet or case you've written for yourself
> - a brag doc, weekly notes, or 1:1 notes if you keep them
> - old resume versions, even bad ones
> - 2–3 job postings for roles you'd actually want
> - anything public: portfolio, GitHub, talks, writing

Then: "Also, is there anything you already know is wrong or missing in your current CV or LinkedIn? Starting from your own diagnosis is usually faster than mine."

## Phase 1 — the brief

- What roles are you going for — actual titles you'd apply to?
- What kind of company: size, stage, industry, sector?
- Are you looking to do more of what you do now, or change something about it?
- What would make you turn a role down?
- Where are you, and what's your situation on location, remote, relocation?
- Anything constraining the search — notice period, visa or work authorisation, compensation floor, industries you can't work in, a current employer who shouldn't see this?
- What's the timeline? Actively applying, or preparing?
- Is there a specific role or company that prompted this?
- **What makes this next role different from your last one?**
- How is the search going so far — applications, responses, interviews? (Tells you whether the problem is the document, the targeting, or the volume.)
- What do you think is going wrong?
- What are you afraid a hiring manager sees when they look at your CV?
- What's the objection you expect to get, and do you think it's fair?

### Phase 1b — establishing the target

With postings in hand:
- Which of these is closest to the job you actually want?
- Which requirement here worries you most?

With only a job family named:
- When you say [analytics engineering / product], what does that look like day to day in your head?
- Here's what I understand roles in that family generally want evidence of: [list]. Does that match what you're seeing in the ads you've been reading, and what would you add or cut?
- Which of those do you already have, and which is the one you're least sure about?
- Are there people you know doing this job? What does their work actually consist of?

## Phase 2 — the spine

Fast factual pass, one employer at a time:

- Employer, what they do, and roughly how big.
- Your title(s) and dates, including internal moves and promotions.
- Location, and whether it was permanent, contract, freelance or founder.
- One line: why you joined, and why you left.

Then confirm the whole spine back, and ask: "Anything missing from that — consulting on the side, a company that got acquired and renamed, a period that's hard to label?"

## Phase 3 — role deep dive

Open with free recall, always:

> Take me back to [company]. Walk me through those [N] years — as much detail as you want, in whatever order it comes. I'll shut up and take notes, and then I'll dig in.

### Context and mandate
- What state was the team/product/function in when you arrived?
- What were you actually hired to do? Was there a problem they wanted solved?
- What did your first ninety days look like?
- What was hard about this job that wouldn't be obvious from the outside?
- Who did you report to, and who reported to you?

### Scope
- How big was the team you led, or worked in? What roles?
- Budget or P&L you controlled? Revenue or spend you influenced?
- Users, customers, accounts, transactions, sites, countries — what numbers describe the thing you worked on?
- Who were your stakeholders, and how senior?
- What did you own outright versus influence?
- How does that compare to the role before it?

### Accomplishments (repeat 3–6 times per major role)
- What are you proudest of from that role?
- What was the biggest thing you shipped, launched, fixed, sold, built or changed?
- Take that one: what was the situation before you started on it?
- What specifically did *you* do — where were your hands?
- Who else was involved, and what was your part?
- What happened as a result? (Then run the metric ladder in `elicitation.md`.)
- How do you know that number — where did it come from?
- What was hard about it? What nearly went wrong?
- Did anyone notice — recognition, promotion, being asked to do it again bigger?
- What did that make possible afterwards?

### Sweep before moving on
- What did you do there that isn't on your resume?
- What would have broken if you hadn't been there?
- What's the thing people still ask you about from that job?
- What did you learn there that you use now?
- If I asked your manager what you were best at, what would they say?

Then read back four or five lines and ask what you got wrong.

## Phase 4 — cross-cutting inventory

Ask what they *use*, never whether the CV is *right* — "does this list still look accurate?" gets a yes and finds nothing.

- What did you actually write / build / work in last month?
- If someone tested you on one thing from your stack tomorrow, what would you pick — and what's on the CV you'd rather they skipped?
- What have you stopped using that's still on your CV?
- What do you use that never made it onto the list?
- Degrees, institutions, dates, and anything notable — honours, societies, thesis, relevant coursework if early-career.
- Certifications and licences, with dates and whether they're current.
- Languages, and honest levels.
- Publications, patents, talks, podcasts, open source, writing.
- Awards, internal or industry.
- Volunteering, board or committee seats, professional bodies, mentoring.
- Side projects — including ones you'd hesitate to list.
- Training you've delivered, or people you've formally mentored.

Then walk the Phase 1b matrix: "Your targets all ask for [X], [Y] and [Z]. I've heard plenty about X. Tell me about Y — and be honest if the answer is that you haven't done it, because that changes strategy rather than the wording."

And on anything they want to cut: "You've said those years aren't relevant. Before we drop them — is there anything you learned there that you still use, or that people in your target field usually don't know?" A candidate's own verdict on their irrelevant history is often wrong in the useful direction.

## Phase 5 — signature and differentiation

- What are you unusually good at, compared to others doing the same job?
- What do people pull you into regardless of your job title?
- What's the kind of problem you'd back yourself to solve that most people in your field wouldn't?
- What's a strongly-held view you have about how this work should be done?
- Describe how you work with a team — and give me a moment that shows it rather than an adjective.
- What's a decision you're proud of, especially an unpopular one?
- Tell me about something that failed. What did you change afterwards?
- What's the pattern across your career that you'd want someone to see?
- After thirty seconds with your profile, what do you want a hiring manager to believe about you?
- What do you *not* want to be hired for anymore?

Executive additions:
- How would you describe your operating philosophy in a sentence?
- What's the largest transformation you've led, and what was the before and after at company level?
- Board, investor, analyst or press exposure?
- Who have you grown — people now in bigger roles because of you?
- What's your public footprint: speaking, writing, industry standing?

## Phase 6 — loose ends

- I see a gap between X and Y — what was happening, and how do you want it handled?
- Any role you'd rather leave off, or present differently?
- Any short stints we should give context to?
- Anything under NDA or confidential that we need to describe generically?
- How do you want your name shown? Pronouns, if you want them listed?
- Contact details, city, links — what goes on a public document versus a CV you send directly?
- Is your current employer likely to see your LinkedIn? Does that constrain anything?
- Anything you'd be uncomfortable being asked about in an interview because of how we've phrased it?

## Phase 7 — synthesis check

After proposing pillars and positioning:

- Does that sound like you, or like a version of you?
- Is that what you want to be hired for, or just what you've been doing?
- Which of those three matters most?
- What have I over-weighted? What have I missed?
- If you could only keep one accomplishment on the page, which one?

## Role-type variants

**Software / engineering.** Systems owned and their scale (traffic, data volume, uptime). Architectural decisions they argued for and the trade-off. Incidents handled. Performance, cost or reliability figures. Code they own versus review. Whether they set technical direction, and for how many people. What they'd rebuild differently.

**Engineering / technical management.** Team state on arrival: size, attrition, morale, delivery record. Hires made and where they came from. People promoted. Process changes and what they measurably moved. Delivery predictability before and after. Budget and vendor decisions. Reorgs survived or led.

**Product.** Products and their numbers — users, revenue, retention, growth. Strategy they set versus inherited. Launches with outcome metrics, and things they killed. Discovery and research practice. Who they had to align, and how senior. A bet they made that paid off, and one that didn't.

**Design / UX.** Portfolio pieces with measured outcomes, not just visuals. Research they ran. Design systems built and their adoption. Stakeholders convinced. Accessibility and scale. Before/after on a flow with the conversion or task-success number.

**Sales / commercial.** Quota and attainment by year. Deal sizes, cycle length, territory and segment. Largest deals named or characterised. Pipeline built. New logos versus expansion. Ranking against peers. Team quota if they managed. President's Club and equivalents.

**Marketing / growth.** Channels owned and spend. CAC, LTV, conversion, pipeline contribution, MQL-to-SQL. Campaigns with results. Brand or positioning work and its effect. Team and agency management.

**Operations / supply chain / manufacturing.** Volume, throughput, sites, headcount. Cost per unit, cycle time, defect or safety rates — before and after. Process or system implementations. Compliance and audit outcomes.

**Finance / accounting.** Scale of books, entities, transaction volume. Audits, closes, reporting cycles and how they improved. Systems implemented. Capital raised, cost savings, forecast accuracy. Regulatory environment.

**Healthcare / clinical.** Patient volume, case mix, setting. Outcome or quality measures. Protocols introduced. Teaching and supervision. Licences, board certification, credentialing.

**Academic / research.** Funding won with amounts. Publication record and venues. Citations if strong. Students supervised. Teaching load and evaluations. Collaborations, editorial and review service. Industry translation.

**Career changers.** What the target field actually requires, line by line. Where the transferable evidence is. Domain knowledge that carries over. Concrete steps already taken into the new field — courses, projects, contracts, conversations. The honest story of why, because it will be asked.

**Early career.** Internships with real responsibility. Substantial coursework or capstone projects, with scale. Part-time and service jobs — quantify them, they demonstrate reliability and volume. Societies and leadership positions. Side projects and self-teaching. Anything with a number attached to it.
